---

The Tomorrow Company's projects and original IP shouldn't look like they all belong
to the same product category. The company itself needs to be the organizing idea.

## Overall

True white should dominate. I'm imagining 90% to 95% of the screen literally being white.

Almost no gray panels, no gradients, no colorful tech graphics. Black type, the rabbit/hole
mark, and perhaps very fine technical lines or small pieces of information.

There should be a lot of space. Nothing should feel crowded or over-designed.

The website shouldn't look technical. It should behave technically. The technology should
come through in how the site moves, responds and reveals things, rather than through
circuit-board graphics, grids, glowing spheres or conventional AI imagery.

## Discovery

The visual interest should come from movement and discovery across the white field.
Somewhere on that large white field is our very small rabbit looking into the hole. At first
it might just seem like part of the identity. Then you realize it isn't decoration.

> *(In the PDF: the mark — a line-drawn rabbit head with two tall ears, seen from the front,
> looking down into a flat black elliptical hole.)*

Move toward it and it responds. Follow it and something is revealed. Enter the hole and
perhaps you reach another layer of the company.

I like the idea of the rabbit/hole becoming an organizing device for the site rather than a
logo sitting in the corner. It communicates curiosity and discovery without having to
explain it.

Projects don't necessarily need to appear in a conventional grid. They can be discovered as
you move through the site. The experience should be extremely simple, but gradually reveal
more as you explore it.

## Interaction

Movement should be unexpected but subtle. No moving just for the sake of movement.
Interaction should reveal something, take you somewhere or make the site feel alive.

It should never become complicated to navigate. Simple to navigate. Interesting to explore.

## Projects

The Tomorrow Company is creating technologies, companies, products, experiences, original IP
and things that don't fit neatly into any existing category.

The site needs to be able to hold all of that. They belong together because they came from
The Tomorrow Company, not because we make them all look the same.

## Design

| Topic | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Typography** | Technical without feeling cold. |
| **Navigation** | Extremely simple. |
| **Motion** | Unexpected but subtle. |
| **Projects** | Different projects should be allowed to have different identities. |
| **Imagery** | I'm not sure how much imagery we need. I'd like to explore whether the restraint itself can be more interesting. |
| **Interaction** | Something should make the site feel alive. |
| **White space** | A lot of it. The space itself should feel intentional. |
| **Language** | Explain enough to understand what we're doing, but not so much that we explain away the curiosity. |
| **Personality** | Intelligent, optimistic, curious and a little unexpected. |
| **What not to do** | To look like a typical AI company or technology startup. No dark sci-fi aesthetic, glowing gradients, generic 3D objects or technology graphics for the sake of looking technological. Nothing so experimental that people have to figure out how to use it. The innovation should be something you feel, not something the website is constantly trying to tell you. |

## The Feeling

At first: This is incredibly simple.

Then: Wait, what's happening here?

And then: I want to see where this goes.

Perhaps we can start by exploring a few different ways the homepage could bring this idea
to life, particularly the white space, the rabbit/hole, movement and the way projects are
discovered, before designing the rest of the site.
