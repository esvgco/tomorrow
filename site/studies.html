04.1411 NovThe wall, and what is under it, to the edge04.13 with its layers rearranged for an iPhone, to compare. Safari cuts everything fixed off under its own bars; what is simply in the document it shows through them. So here nothing is fixed: the document is held still, the wall lies in it and reaches over the window’s edges, and a box over the window does the scrolling (after the Orbit project). Only on iOS; ?layer=inner forces it elsewhere.
04.1311 NovThe wall, and what is under it, steady04.12 with two more things, to compare. Nothing is drawn into a texture while the wall moves: the rabbit is two pictures that never change, and the falling letters are one small picture per character. And the camera follows the scroll with a short lag instead of being pinned to it, which evens out the steps a phone reports unevenly (?ease=0 pins it again, ?ease=120 is softer).
04.1211 NovThe wall, and what is under it, lean04.11 with nothing left for the browser to do while it moves, to compare. The rabbit and the mist are drawn in WebGL too; moving, the wall is drawn at three quarters of the resolution and at rest at all of it; the loop stops when nothing moves; the chapters in the page are not touched until the pointer comes down, and a click goes to what is seen under it, through the lens. Add ?perf to either for a readout.
04.1111 NovThe wall, and what is under it04.10 taken further. The walk never stands still: the rabbit goes down as the last screen arrives, and its hole lets go of the window and travels on with the wall. What the rabbit found comes out of the hole: three projects squeezed through it and thrown down on the sheet, each a title and one line. A project is larger than the wall, not deeper in it: opening one steps back until the wall sits small in the corner of its page; any scroll, Back, Escape or a touch of the small wall steps forward again. The door above the first screen opens on black, and in the black is the other side of the wall: the studio in rooms one behind another, walked forward and out into the next day. Lighter on a phone: chapters are drawn once, nothing is read from the page while scrolling, and the toolbar coming and going changes nothing. Project pages are draft copy.
04.1011 NovThe wall, through a lens, in WebGL04.9 drawn another way, to compare. The chapters stay in the page, invisible; each is drawn into a texture at the size it has on screen, and a shader bends the edges. It works in Safari, and the bend is deeper and smooth. Not carried over yet: hover states, the caret of the edit.
04.911 NovThe wall, through a lens04.6 with one addition: the top and bottom of the window are a lens. What passes them flares out like a bell; the middle stays as it is, and the rabbit stands in front of it. ?lens=1.5 bends more.

04.65 NovThe wall, from afarThe ending of 04.1, played backwards: it opens on the whole wall from far away and walks you in to the last screen, with the rabbit waiting at the foot of the window. There it goes into its hole; the hole becomes the next screen, rising and growing like the others, and the projects arrive one after another. Last: About and Contact. Or scroll up from the start: the rabbit drops into its hole, the wall slides down as if you had raised your head, and above it there is one word on a white screen you can draw on. Hatch the middle and a door appears that the ink will not take; press its handle.

065 NovThe portalTheir first screen. Down: their site; the lead falls into a list of five words and on into the next paragraph, and while it is a list, the space between its letters is a door into a project's own space (hello · back). Up: the head goes into the hole, the hole opens into a circle, the circle fills the screen and runs black → blue → orange → lilac → white, and the same content is there again, placed in a room and scrolled forward. Up is forward, down is back; scroll down past the start, or Esc, to return.
